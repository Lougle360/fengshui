# Editorial policy

## Source-derived content

Modern translations, retained quotations, source headings, captions, and notes keep source block IDs. Reordering requires explicit provenance and must not change argumentative sequence.

## Editor-created content

Label additions as `编者导读`, `编者注`, `背景说明`, `术语说明`, or `阅读提示`. Record sources for factual background when available. Never present generated background as part of the original author’s text.

## Default reading style

- Simplified Chinese, horizontal layout, modern punctuation.
- Moderate paragraph length and descriptive headings only when labeled or source-supported.
- Explain a technical term on first occurrence; use the approved term consistently afterward.
- Preserve uncertainty notes where they affect meaning; move purely technical OCR details to the audit appendix.
- Avoid sensational summaries, present-day moral judgments, and unrequested fact checking inside the reading text.

## Ordinary-reader edition

- Make every intervention answer a reader problem: use `增` for necessary context and examples, `删` for repetition and pipeline clutter in the reading path, `改` for clear modern expression, and `整` for navigable structure. Preserve displaced source material and provenance.
- Open with a plain-language book introduction and reading map before the original preface/body.
- Make the introduction explain what the surviving book is, what it contains, why it matters to this reader, how its parts relate, what is distinctive, where to begin, what remains uncertain, and what this edition changed.
- Introduce each major chapter with the question it answers and the concepts the reader needs.
- Define a specialist term at first occurrence, use it consistently, and include it in the end glossary.
- Give core glossary terms a plain explanation, book-specific use, exact source occurrence, example, related concepts, and common-confusion warning. A circular definition or another unexplained technical label does not count.
- Prefer concrete examples and observation sequences over abstract paraphrase when the source permits them.
- Keep a clean continuous reading path; place dense case catalogues and lookup material in clearly labeled reference sections when appropriate.

## Traditional claims

Preserve traditional doctrine but attribute it. Use phrases such as `书中认为`, `传统上认为`, and `古人以为` when a claim could be mistaken for contemporary verified fact. Distinguish visible form or recorded practice from causal, predictive, medical, or fortune interpretations. Editorial notes may explain this distinction but may not rewrite the author into a modern scientific voice.

## Figures and tables

Do more than retain an image. Tell the reader what it represents, where to begin, what sequence to follow, and which nearby paragraph it supports. Do not infer missing labels. If a figure is unreadable, send it back to reconstruction and record the limitation.

## What editing cannot repair

Editing cannot cure corrupt OCR, omitted pages, broken reading order, hallucinated characters, or unsupported translation. Return these defects to the responsible evidence stage instead of smoothing them into plausible prose.
